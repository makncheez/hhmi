Understanding how brains generate behavior.

Big questions. Long horizons. Sustained support.

Four units.
Connected scientific work.

Integrated teams of biologists, theorists, tool builders and AI researchers work together to pursue Janelia’s overarching mission and move biological discoveries forward across HHMI and in the larger scientific community.

Biology pursues the central scientific research questions at the heart of Janelia’s mission. Theory@HHMI guides these questions, developing conceptual frameworks that can be tested in the lab. Tools@HHMI enables this work, building the microscopes, molecular tools, and instruments that drive biological discovery. AI@HHMI powers this research, creating AI systems that help scientists analyze and understand their data and eventually design and run experiments as well.

Theory, Tools, and AI set their research priorities in dialogue with Biology’s scientific questions, and with one another. Together, they operate as a single, integrated enterprise united by and working toward a common vision.

Mission

HHMI’s Janelia Research Campus in Ashburn, Virginia, is undertaking an ambitious, decade-long challenge to understand how a complex brain generates behavior, and to develop a new mode of discovery in which biologists and AI work together to drive science forward.

Journey

After centuries of investigation, scientists still cannot explain how the brain integrates sensory information, internal states, memories, and physiology to produce behaviors. Understanding the fundamental function of the brain is vital not only in its own right, but as the key to making real progress against brain disorders, like Alzheimer’s disease and depression, that impact hundreds of millions of people worldwide.

Janelia’s grand challenge is to build a mechanistic account connecting molecules, neural circuits, brain activity, and complex behavior in a whole animal in motion.

Two forces have converged to bring what was previously impossible within reach: a new generation of tools that lets us observe whole biological systems with a completeness that wasn’t possible a decade ago, and AI capable of parsing data at scales no human scientist can manage. The identification of a new experimental model, Danionella — a small fish that stays transparent into adulthood — also now gives researchers a way to see the whole brain at work.

Seizing the moment requires exactly what Janelia was built to provide: sustained support, shared infrastructure, interdisciplinary teams, and the time to ask the hard questions.

Why

With small labs and large ambitions, Janelia welcomes biologists, AI scientists, physicists, chemists, engineers, theorists, and computational scientists to work side by side on groundbreaking research.

Internal funding, expert technical teams, and world-class facilities are all on one campus, giving Janelia scientists the intellectual freedom to take on big risks, and the sustained support to see them through.

Janelia’s three lead scientist roles — Principal Scientists, AI Scientists, and Group Leaders — bring complementary forms of scientific leadership and take intellectual responsibility for their work. All three feature stable internal funding and minimal administrative duties. Each spends their time doing science and working with collaborators across campus.

Janelia’s three lead scientist roles

Janelia’s ambitious mission needs scientists who work in different ways.

Principal Scientist

  • Develop approaches and contribute deep expertise to shared questions.
  • A distinct career centered on direct scientific contribution.
  • Contribute intellectual leadership across projects and disciplines.

On-Site / Internally funded / No postdoc required

Learn more about Principal Scientist roles

AI Scientist

  • Develop AI methods and systems connected to scientific experiments.
  • Computational research, modeling, and scientific systems.
  • Connect computational approaches with biologists, theorists, and tool builders.
Learn more about AI Scientist roles

Group Leader

  • Build a laboratory around broad questions and a portfolio of projects.
  • The balance of research and team leadership depends on the role and program.
  • Lead a lab and collaborate across the shared scientific enterprise.
Learn more about Group Leader roles

Project Teams

Some scientific projects are too big for one lab to accomplish on its own, but have the potential to transform science. For these projects, Janelia created Project Teams: dedicated groups of scientists who come together to pursue a specialized goal.
